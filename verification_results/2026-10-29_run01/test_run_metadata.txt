TEST OBJECTIVE
--------------
Verify that the Red Pitaya analog input can acquire frequencies through the
expected 9.3 MHz Doppler carrier range. Verify end-to-end transmission of a
prerecorded IVUS dataset from the Red Pitaya ARM processor to the Raspberry Pi
with measurable throughput, packet-integrity checking, and CRC validation.

TEST DEVIATIONS OR ANOMALIES
----------------------------
The Red Pitaya static IPv4 address was assigned manually for the test session.
The logger began its timer with the first valid DDSP IVUS packet.
A final partial frame existed when the 300-second timer expired and was
correctly excluded rather than classified as a damaged frame.
The one-second throughput samples alternate because a 3.333333 frame/s source
places either three or four complete frames into individual one-second bins.
This test used prerecorded IVUS dataset playback and does not claim live IVUS
probe acquisition.

CONCLUSION
----------
The Red Pitaya successfully acquired all six commanded analog frequencies
through 9.3 MHz within the proposed frequency and amplitude limits. During the
five-minute prerecorded IVUS streaming test, the Red Pitaya-to-Raspberry Pi
UDP path delivered 1,000 CRC-valid frames with no detected missing packets,
malformed packets, duplicate packets, damaged frames, or CRC failures.
Measured mean throughput matched the calculated theoretical wire rate.

These results verify the tested analog acquisition and prerecorded IVUS
network-transport subsystems. They do not yet verify live IVUS acquisition,
live Doppler demodulation, flow velocity, or cardiac-output calculation.
